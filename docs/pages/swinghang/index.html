A Swing Has to Be Earned

From a dead hang she only rocksevery pass through the bottom builds on the last

Hanging still against a wall, holding a direction carried the doll most of the way to horizontal in one go. Leaning pushed her sideways with a fixed force, as if someone was shoving her, so it didn't matter that she had no room or speed to build a swing.

Now a lean behaves like a child pumping a swing. It pushes along the arc, its strength grows with how fast she is already swinging, and it fades out near horizontal. From a dead hang the first swing is tiny, and the swing grows only by leaning in time, pass after pass.

Hanging off a cliff

When I hang off the edge of a cliff with a wall on one side and nothing on the other I can “swing” to almost like 90 degrees even though I have no space to start the swing. It should be impossible, the first swing should be only super short and only when I come to the other side it gets larger, like the real swing.

This is a physics change, so the page uses numbers instead of screenshots. Everything below comes from the game's own physics. In the test, the doll walks off the east edge of the first Bell Gallery pillar, grips while falling, waits until she hangs still against the pillar's face, then leans away from the wall.

With the wall beside her, every swing back runs into the rock and loses its speed, so she can't build up. That's what a real wall does to a real swing.

In open air, from a dead hang

BeforeAfter

Hanging still under a ring with clear air on both sides and leaning in time with the swing: before, the first swing went to 46° and the second went over the top of the ring. Now the swings grow 6°, 16°, 31°, 52°, 54°, then 81°. Holding one direction the whole time, without timing it, now does almost nothing, where before it reached 47°.

  • How the lean works now. The push points along the arc instead of straight sideways, so a lean can never pull her against the thread. Its strength rises with her swing speed and reaches full power at 8 m/s; from a standstill she gets 6% of it. It also fades with the angle, so near horizontal leaning can't add height. Leaning against the swing still brakes it.
  • Walking off a ledge with grip held used to leave her balanced on the rounded corner with the thread pulled flat. Now the thread keeps paying out while she rolls over the lip, so she drops into a hang under the edge. That's the situation you described, and it works from either a walk-off or a grip in mid-fall.

Every ring gap still works

All 12 gaps were simulated again: run-up, jump, catch, let-go at every time from 0.1 to 6 s, with grip pressed early or at the ring, and with holding forward or leaning in time. Every gap still has let-go windows in all four styles. A catch keeps a quarter of her jump speed, which is enough for the new lean to work with, so crossing a gap feels much as before. The let-go window is a little later, because the first swing builds more slowly.
