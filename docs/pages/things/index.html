What changed

  • Modelled, not drawn. Yarn is real strands: every stitch of the knitting is a loop of it, and the ball is wound turn by turn. Both have fibres that have worked loose, and ends that fray.
  • Worn. Wood, metal, horn, leather and paper are each made of their own grain, scratches, dirt in the hollows and bare edges where hands have been.
  • Printed and written. Everything lettered is on the thing itself, so you can read it there.
  • Lit for looking at. A warm light from the upper left, a cold edge from behind, and polished things reflect a dark room. Everything behind them goes quiet while one is held up, and the bright haze that washed them out is gone.
  • Finer lines. Close up, the engraving is finer and lies over a continuous tone, so it still looks printed but the detail stays.
  • Yours to turn. Drag with the mouse to turn one and see its back. A click without dragging, or any key, puts it away as before.
  • No waiting. A model is loaded when you come within 45 metres of the thing. The old drawing stands in if one is not there yet.

Checked

All nine were shown one after another in the running game, with no errors. With one held up the game ran at 58 frames a second. Turning one by dragging kept it up, and a click then put it away. Three keepsakes and a skein were looked at where they lie.
